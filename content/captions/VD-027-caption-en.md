Twenty seconds left on the red light. Your phone is already out.

Three people ahead of you at the till. Phone out. Kettle coming up to the boil. Out again. It's not that something's wrong with you — it's twenty seconds, what else were you going to do? But a day is full of them. And every single one, you spend the same way.

You're not short on time. You're just looking down at the exact moment there's something to see.

So try it once. Red light. Leave the phone in your pocket. Look up. The rain stopped a few minutes ago and the road is still shining with it. At the stall on the corner, someone is stacking the little plastic stools — same hands, same order, without once looking down. A kid on the back of a bike, head tipped over, fast asleep.

Not all of it is lovely. Some days you look up and it's exhaust fumes and horns and nothing else. That's fine too. The point was never the view. It's that for twenty seconds you were actually here.

That waiting time is already yours. It's built into the day. Nobody has to give it to you.

Next time you wait today — a red light, a queue, the kettle — leave the phone in your pocket, just once. Then tell me what you saw.

Small things, said quietly. A new one every couple of days.
