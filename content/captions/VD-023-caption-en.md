## 2 · Mô tả — copy khối này

```
Who cooked your dinner tonight? That food didn't get warm on its own.

You get home and dinner is there. Every single day. So you stopped seeing it.

For rice to be warm at seven, somebody plugged the cooker in at six. You were still out on the road. Somebody went to the market. Somebody remembered you can't take it too spicy. You only ever see the last bit — the food already on the table.

Nothing is ordinary by itself. Someone makes it ordinary.

And it isn't only dinner. The lunchbox pushed into your bag on the way out this morning. Even the noodle place on your street — how many times now have they got your bowl exactly right? You've never once thought: they remember.

None of this is here to make you feel guilty. The person cooking isn't sitting there waiting for a thank you. They cook because they want you fed. That's the whole thing.

But doing the same thing for years with nobody ever mentioning it — some evenings that does feel like nothing at all. One sentence undoes that. It costs nothing and it takes two seconds.

Not a speech. Just name the thing they did: this is really good tonight.

Cooking for yourself tonight? Then that sentence is yours. You got yourself a warm meal today, and that counts.

Tonight, when you've finished eating, say that one line to whoever cooked. Just the one line.

Small things, said quietly. A new one every couple of days.
```
