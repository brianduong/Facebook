## 2 · Mô tả — copy khối này

```
What do you stay up an extra hour for? Not work. Not a book. Just the phone, until your eyes close on their own. There's a name for it — revenge bedtime procrastination. You didn't get one minute to yourself all day, so you take it back at night by refusing to go to sleep.

And honestly, it makes sense. From the moment you opened your eyes, the day belonged to somebody else. Work, messages, other people's things. That hour at midnight is the first thing all day that nobody is asking you for.

But look at what you actually get. Not rest — by morning you can't even remember what you were looking at. And what it costs is tomorrow. Short on sleep, you snap faster, small things land heavier, everything feels a little more personal. So tomorrow isn't yours either, and tomorrow night you'll want to take it back again. That's the loop.

The way out isn't more discipline at midnight. Nobody has discipline at midnight. Take your hour earlier — early evening, while you're still awake enough to choose. Twenty minutes is fine, as long as it's actually yours. Going to bed isn't giving up on your day. It's the first kind thing you do for the you of tomorrow.

So set the alarm backwards tonight. Not one that wakes you up. One that tells you to go to bed.

Small things, said quietly. One video a day.
```
