## 2 · Mô tả — copy khối này

```
In your head, that thing is brilliant. So good you don't dare start it.

Because starting means it has to turn into an actual thing. And an actual thing never comes out looking like the one in your head. So you put it off — and you don't call it fear. You call it waiting till you've got time, waiting till you've prepared properly, waiting till you feel like it.

The draft you wrote really is clumsy. But it's on paper. The good one is still in your imagination.

The good one in your head doesn't count. Nobody can read it, nobody can eat it, nobody can use it. A badly cooked dinner is still dinner.

None of this means throw something together and call it done. It's that a bad version can be fixed — you can see which bits are clumsy, so you can work on those bits. There's nothing to fix in the one in your head.

Every channel's first video is bad. Every first book makes the writer wince years later. They got further because back then they were willing to make the bad one.

What you're actually scared of isn't the bad thing. It's seeing the gap between what your eye knows is good and what your hands can make. Everyone has that gap, and it only narrows while your hands are moving.

So make the bad version today. One page is enough. Something ugly is enough. Then tell me what you just made badly — I'll go first, mine's pinned at the top of the comments.

Small things, said quietly. A new one every couple of days.
```

## Posting notes

- ❗ **Required: pin the confession comment as soon as the video goes live.** The CTA only
  works if the channel goes first. Copy this as the pinned comment:

  ```
  I'll go first, to be fair. This channel's first video was read by my computer's built-in robot voice, dry as a station announcement, and the script was written in English and then bent into shape, so it never sounded like a person talking. I still wince at it. But if I'd waited until I could make a decent one, there'd be no videos at all. What did you make badly today?
  ```

- **Don't critique what people confess.** Someone will name the thing they just made
  badly — don't offer tips on doing it better unless they ask. The job here is to say
  *you made it*, and stop there. Critiquing is exactly what the video argues against.
- **Don't let it become "so just be sloppy".** If someone reads it that way, reply with
  the actual line: a bad version is worth making **because it can be fixed**.
- Different from VD-013 (the first ten minutes): that one is about the difficulty of
  *starting*; this one is about the fear of the *result*. Don't mention ten minutes or
  breaking work into chunks.
