## 2 · Mô tả — copy khối này

```
Someone tells you "good job". You've forgotten it before you reach the door.

But there's one compliment from years back you still have. You remember who said it. You remember where you were standing. The difference is one thing: that one named the thing you had actually done. Whoever said it wasn't calling you clever — they were describing something they had watched you do.

"Good job" fits anybody. You can say it without having looked at all.

A specific compliment isn't really about the work. It's proof somebody sat and looked at all of it.

A colleague finishes a deck — don't stop at "looks great", say which page. Your kid hands you their homework — don't stop at "well done", say which part. People go quiet for a second when you do this. Not because of the praise. Because somebody looked that closely.

This doesn't mean every sentence has to be long. "Good job" still works, especially when you're rushing. It's just this: once in a day, pick one person and finish the sentence.

And to say something specific you have to look first. That's the hard part. Not the saying.

Small things, said quietly. A new one every couple of days.
```

## Posting notes

- **Don't let it become a rule against "good job".** The script says outright that the short
  version still works when you're rushing. If comments head that way, pull back to that line.
- **Avoid the "thank you, because…" frame from VD-004** in replies. The two episodes share a
  mechanism (making words specific) and the idea passed review only on that condition. This
  one uses a different frame: **name the thing they did**.
- **Avoid "recognition", "positive energy", "spread the love", "inspiring"** in copy, tags and
  replies.
- **The likely pushback:** "isn't that just flattery with extra words?" Answer with the hard
  part the script names — **you have to look first**, so it can't be flattery.
- The previous episode (VD-029, 7 Sep) asked people to pick a time for themselves; this one
  asks for a sentence said to someone else. No repeat.
- **Expect real stories in the comments** — people tend to quote the compliment they still
  remember. That's good raw material for the VD-037+ batch; read them and write them down.
- Select "No, it's not made for kids" and **People & Blogs**.
- Keep `Photos: Pexels.com`; don't add another music track.
