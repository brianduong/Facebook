## 2 · Mô tả — copy khối này

```
The last time you had a go at yourself — did that sentence sound familiar? Whose voice was it?

Try to remember the actual words. Not the gist — the words.

For a lot of people the words come back old. "Can't even get that right." "You never finish anything." They sound like something from twenty years ago. And you've never said that to anyone. A friend messes something up and you don't talk to them like that.

You didn't come up with that sentence. You're playing it back.

This isn't about finding someone to blame. Whoever said it years ago has probably forgotten. They might have been worn out that day, and it fell out of them without landing anywhere on purpose.

And knowing whose words they are doesn't make them wrong. Some of it lands — there are places you do leave things half done, and those you fix. The problem is that the sentence never gets checked. It arrives in a grown-up voice, so you believe it and ask nothing.

Catching it is easier than you'd think: listen to the words. Your own sentence talks about a thing you did. A borrowed one talks about the whole of you. "I rushed this report" is yours. "I rush everything" is borrowed. One points at the work. The other points at the person.

You'll say fine, I know where it came from — it still runs. It does. But it runs in a different position. Before, it was a verdict. Now it's a sentence with an owner, and the owner isn't in the room. A sentence with an owner can be looked at again. A verdict can't.

Small things, said quietly. A new one every couple of days.
```
