## 2 · Mô tả — copy khối này

```
Someone starts telling you something hard. And halfway through, your head has already written the answer. It usually starts the same way: "What you should do is…"

You're not being rude. You're trying to help. That's the whole reason it's queued up.

But look where they are. They've got about four minutes of this in them, and you're standing at minute two.

They didn't come for the answer. They came to finish the sentence.

And here's the odd part. Let them run to minute four and they usually find their own way out — the same answer you had, but it arrives as theirs, so they'll actually do it. Cut in at minute two and they get your answer instead. It's a good answer. It just sits there.

This isn't a ban on advice. Sometimes they ask you straight out, and then you answer straight out. It's about waiting to be asked — two more minutes of waiting, most of the time.

And you don't have to sit there mute. "And then?" "That does sound heavy." Or nothing at all — just don't reach for your phone. And leave three seconds after they stop; most people aren't finished, they're just breathing.

You know this from the other side. You've been the one still talking at minute two.

Small things, said quietly. A new one every couple of days.

```

## Posting notes

- **Never use "active listening", "empathetic listening", "connection", "healing", "energy"**
  in copy, tags or replies. **"Active/empathetic listening" especially** — it turns this into
  a soft-skills lecture, which is exactly the failure mode flagged for this idea.
- ⚠️ **This is not a dig at people who give advice.** The person cutting in **genuinely wants
  to help** — that line sits early in the script; keep it in replies. If the comments turn
  into complaints about people who lecture, pull back to it.
- ⚠️ **It's also not a ban on advice.** If someone asks you straight out, answer straight out.
  It's about waiting to be asked.
- ⚠️ **Keep the numbers consistent: four minutes of it · cutting in at minute two · two more
  minutes of waiting · three seconds of silence.**
- **The usable part is the actual wording** — *"and then?"*, *"that does sound heavy"*, and
  the three seconds. The video hands people something to say rather than only telling them
  what not to say; replies should do the same.
- **This is the mirror of VD-016** (*ask one more question*, 11 Aug): that one **adds** a
  question, this one **subtracts** a piece of advice. They pair up for a long-form pillar-5
  cut. **Not VD-024** (*people who are drowning don't ask*, 28 Aug) — that one is arriving
  before you're called; this is staying quiet longer once you're already sitting there.
- ⚠️ **VD-028 (9 Sep) is also a Reaching-people episode** — five slots apart on purpose.
- Select "No, it's not made for kids" and **People & Blogs**.
- Keep `Photos: Pexels.com`; don't add another music track.
