## 2 · Mô tả — copy khối này

```
Three in the morning. You're replaying a decision from two years ago. And you already know the verdict.

Same sentence every time. I should have known.

Here's the strange bit. When you replay it, you can see how it ended. You know exactly what that choice cost. The person in that memory didn't. They were standing in a half-lit room, and they had to pick a door.

You are judging them with a file they never had.

This isn't a way out of it. Some decisions really were careless, and you knew it at the time. Those ones you fix.

So there's one question worth asking. At that moment, with only what was on the table then — was it a stupid call? Most of the time the honest answer is no. It was a reasonable call that turned out badly.

A bad outcome and a bad decision are not the same thing. You can do it right and still lose.

Your head mixes them up on purpose. Because if every loss was your fault, then next time is still under your control. It feels like holding the wheel. It's really just a way to keep paying for something that already closed.

And yes — sometimes the signs were there. You'll say you saw them at the time. You can see them now because you know where to look. Back then they were sitting in a pile with forty other things that turned out to mean nothing.

So next time the replay starts, don't argue with the verdict. Just add one line: here's what I actually knew that day.

Small things, said quietly. A new one every couple of days.
```
