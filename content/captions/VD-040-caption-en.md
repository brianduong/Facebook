## 2 · Mô tả — copy khối này

```
Three days now and you haven't touched that task. And you already have a word for yourself.

Lazy. The word gets there before the task does.

Here's the strange bit. That word sounds like an answer — once you've said it, the matter feels settled. But look at the same person last week. Six hours straight on something else, nobody pushing.

Lazy isn't the answer. It's where you stopped asking.

This isn't a way out of it. Some days you really are just being lazy — it's urgent and you watch three episodes anyway. You know when that's what it is.

The thing you've been circling for months is different. For that one, there's a better question: what's the first step? Ask it and one of two things usually falls out. One: nobody ever cut that task up. "Sort out the paperwork" isn't one task, it's eleven stuck together, and you haven't named a single one. Heads don't start lumps. Two: you already know the first version will be bad, and while you haven't started, the version in your head is still perfect.

Neither of those is laziness. One is a task that was never cut up. The other is fear. They need completely different fixes — and the word "lazy" points at neither.

That word doesn't tell you what to do next. It only tells you what kind of person you are. Tell someone off and they still don't know where to start.

Small things, said quietly. A new one every couple of days.
```
