The last time the whole table talked about someone who wasn't there — what did you say?

Someone starts. Someone adds a bit. Then someone else. You didn't start it — you just nodded, laughed, and added one line to keep it going.

The person who isn't there is the only one in the conversation who doesn't get to say a word.

Sometimes people really do get it wrong. Then say it to them, or to someone who can fix it. Telling the whole table doesn't fix anything.

You don't have to take on the whole table. Just don't add to it. Or say one true thing about them. Often that's enough, and the conversation turns somewhere else.

Next time, ask yourself quietly: would I say this if they were here?
