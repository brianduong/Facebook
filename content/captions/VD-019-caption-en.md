## 2 · Mô tả — copy khối này

That job you were complaining about this morning. There was a day you sat staring at your phone, just hoping they'd ring and say you got it.

That day you even promised yourself: if I get this one, I won't ask for anything else. Then you got it. Then you got used to it. Then this morning you sat there complaining.

That room you call cramped was once the first room whose key was yours. The person you argued with this morning — there was a stretch when you watched your phone all evening waiting for them to text back.

It's not that there's something wrong with you. Heads just work like that: once a thing is yours, it stops getting counted. It only counts what's missing, so the list in your head is always made of things you don't have yet.

None of this is me telling you to stop complaining. Cramped is still cramped. Tiring is still tiring. If you're fed up, you're fed up, and nobody gets to argue with that.

It's just — now and then, spare a thought for the one who used to want this. The one who sat wishing for exactly the seat you're sitting in. Not so you feel guilty. So that one gets to know the waiting wasn't wasted.

And here's the other half: what you're wishing for right now will also just turn into normal in a couple of years. So if you're waiting until you have it to feel like it's enough, that day doesn't really arrive.

Look up and around the room. Find one thing the old you was hoping for. Then say one line to them: you got it.

Small things, said quietly. A new one every couple of days.

## Posting notes

- **Avoid "gratitude", "grateful", "blessed", "count your blessings"** in the copy, the tags
  and the replies. This is the episode most likely to slide into a gratitude quote, and
  sliding there costs it everything that makes it its own video.
- ⚠️ **Never reply with "so stop complaining".** The idea review banned that ending on
  purpose. People will describe real tiredness — acknowledge it first. The video's line is
  *you're allowed to be fed up, just spare a thought for the one who used to want this*.
- The CTA asks people to do it during the video, so expect short concrete comments ("this
  desk", "this bike"). Reply to each, and ask one question: **how long did you want it?**
- Different from VD-014 (the things that aren't broken today): that one runs on noise —
  broken things shout, working things stay quiet. This one runs on time, and its character
  is the earlier version of you.
- Select "No, it's not made for kids" and **People & Blogs**.
- Keep `Photos: Pexels.com`; don't add another music track.
