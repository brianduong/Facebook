It's pouring, and the person next to you has no umbrella. What do you do?

You're both under the same shop awning. They keep checking the sky. Their bag is held over their head.

You have an umbrella. A big one. And you're going the same way.

But they're a stranger. It'd be awkward. So you open it, and walk off alone.

A little way down the street, you look back. They're still standing there. The rain shows no sign of stopping.

Half an umbrella costs you nothing. You get a bit wet on one shoulder. That's it.

For them, it's the difference between getting home soaked and getting home fine.

Most of us were once walked across a road by a stranger with an umbrella. You don't remember their face. You remember the umbrella.

The easiest kindness is sharing what you already have more than enough of.

A seat on the bus you're about to leave. The extra charger cable. The second pen in your bag.

You don't have to walk off with a stranger if it doesn't feel safe. Late at night, alone — trust that.

Just ask once. Going this way? They can say no. That's fine.

Most people won't say no. And they'll remember the stranger with the umbrella longer than you think.

Next time it rains, look at who's standing next to you.

One wet shoulder. That's all it costs.
