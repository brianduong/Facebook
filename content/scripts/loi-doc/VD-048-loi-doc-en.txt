The last time you were stuck behind someone slow at the checkout, what did you do?

An older man counting out coins. A learner driver stalling as the light turns green. Someone whose payment code just won't scan.

Behind them: sighs. A horn. Someone saying out loud, can you hurry up.

The person at the front hears all of it. The more they hear, the more their hands shake. And the slower it goes.

Everyone gets to be the slow one sometimes. A new machine. A strange city. Or just a messy day.

Take your time won't make the line move faster. But it steadies the person at the front.

Some days you really are in a hurry. Late to pick up the kids. About to miss the bus.

Then be in a hurry quietly. You don't have to say anything. Just don't sigh out loud.

You'll think: what difference does one line from a stranger make?

When you're fumbling in front of a crowd, a stranger's voice is the one you hear clearest.

They might still remember it that evening: someone told me to take my time today.

Next time you're behind someone who's struggling, try not checking the time.

Just say it quietly: take your time, it's okay.
