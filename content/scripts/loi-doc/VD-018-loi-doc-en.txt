In your head, that thing is brilliant. So good you don't dare start it.

Because starting means it has to turn into an actual thing. And an actual thing never comes out looking like the one in your head.

So you put it off. And you don't call it fear. You call it waiting till you've got time, waiting till you've prepared properly, waiting till you feel like it.

The draft you wrote really is clumsy. But it's on paper. The good one is still in your imagination.

The bad version is the only real one.

The good one in your head doesn't count. It isn't anywhere yet. Nobody can read it, nobody can eat it, nobody can use it.

A badly cooked dinner is still dinner. The soup's too salty, the fish is a bit burnt, and everyone still eats.

None of this means throw something together and call it done.

It's that a bad version can be fixed. You can see which bits are clumsy, so you can work on those bits. There's nothing to fix in the one in your head.

Every channel's first video is bad. Every first book makes the writer wince years later. They got further because back then they were willing to make the bad one.

What you're actually scared of isn't the bad thing. It's seeing the gap between what your eye knows is good and what your hands can make.

Everyone has that gap. People who've been at it for years have it too. They've just stood inside it longer than you have.

And it only narrows while your hands are moving. Another month of thinking about it leaves it exactly where it was.

So make the bad version today. One page is enough. Something ugly is enough.
