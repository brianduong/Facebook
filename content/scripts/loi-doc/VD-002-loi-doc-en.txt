There's something kind you did years ago. It's still going. You just never found out.

Here's the story. It's raining hard. Someone's waiting at a bus stop. Next to them, a stranger with no umbrella, getting soaked.

So they hold their umbrella over both of them. Ten minutes. The bus comes, they go different ways. Neither one asks the other's name.

The stranger was on the way to a job interview. They walked in dry, hair still neat. And they got the job.

The person with the umbrella never found out. Still doesn't know.

We skip small kind things all the time. Something that small can't change anything — that's what we tell ourselves.

But kindness doesn't work like a payment you sit and count. It moves. Through one person, then another, into places you'll never see.

The smallest thing you do today might be the one that travels furthest.

So the question isn't whether it's worth doing. The question is: is there someone near you today who needs a small thing?

Kindness is never wasted. Not even when you never see where it lands.
