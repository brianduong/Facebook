Some days you're tired before you've done a thing. Count how many undecided things are hanging in your head.

Should you change jobs. Are you going home this weekend. Do you take that shirt back. Do you reply to that person, or let it go.

None of them is urgent today. But every time you pass one, you pull it out and think it through again. Then you put it back: I'll deal with it tomorrow.

Five things like that, a few times a day, for three weeks. That's where the tired comes from.

An undecided thing doesn't sit still. Every day, it makes you think it through from the start.

I'm not saying decide everything tonight. Some things really can't be decided yet. You're waiting on a result, or you need to ask someone first.

For those, decide something smaller: the day you'll decide. Write that date down. Until then, you're allowed to stop thinking about it.

You'll say: what if I decide and it's the wrong call?

Most of these don't have a right answer hiding somewhere. Go home or stay — either is fine.

What wears you down isn't picking the wrong one. It's three weeks of picking neither.

Decide just one, even a small one, and you'll feel it straight away. A corner of your head goes quiet.

So tonight, write down everything that's hanging. All of it.

Pick the smallest and decide it tonight. If one's too big, write a date next to it: that's the day you'll decide.
