Your boss says: can you pop by my office later? What's the first thing your head does?

That report. It must be the report. Or the late email last week. Or maybe it's worse.

For the next hour, you can't work. You rehearse your defence. You go back over every mistake you've made this month.

Then you go in. He wants to know if you can cover the meeting on Friday.

Your head didn't lie to you. It just picked the worst version first. It does that to keep you safe.

But you paid for it. A whole hour of worry, for something that never happened.

It's not just bosses. Your mum: call me tonight. Your partner: we need to talk. The shorter the message, the longer the story your head writes.

Worrying early doesn't make it lighter. It only makes it longer.

Sometimes it is bad news. That happens.

Then you'll deal with it when you're in the room. You'll still have time. The hour before didn't help you prepare.

Next time a vague message lands, try one of two things.

Ask straight away: what's it about? Or tell yourself, out loud if you have to: I don't know anything yet.

Not knowing yet isn't the same as bad news.
