Someone tells you "good job". You've forgotten it before you reach the door.

But there's one compliment from years back you still have. You remember who said it. You remember where you were standing.

The difference is one thing. That one named the thing you had actually done.

Whoever said it wasn't calling you clever. They were describing something they had watched you do.

"Good job" fits anybody. You can say it without having looked at all.

A specific compliment isn't really about the work. It's proof somebody sat and looked at all of it.

A colleague finishes a deck. Don't stop at "looks great". Say which page.

"That comparison page — I got the whole thing in one look. You must have spent time on that one."

Your kid hands you their homework. Don't stop at "well done". Say which part.

"The bit where you explained why — I could follow that one all the way through."

People go quiet for a second when you do this. It catches them off guard.

Not because of the praise. Because somebody looked that closely.

This doesn't mean every sentence has to be long. "Good job" still works, especially when you're rushing.

It's just this. Once in a day, pick one person and finish the sentence.

And to say something specific you have to look first. That's the hard part. Not the saying.

Today, praise one person and name the thing they did. Don't stop at "good job".
