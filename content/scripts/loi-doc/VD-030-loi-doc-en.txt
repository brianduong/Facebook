Last time you posted a photo — how many did you take before you picked one?

Seven? Fifteen? And then you deleted nearly all of them.

The caption too. Typed, deleted, typed again. Three goes before it sounded like you.

Nobody made you do any of that. You just did it, and it felt completely normal.

The clearest proof that everyone online is a cut version is sitting in your own camera roll.

Nobody sees the fourteen you deleted. They see the fifteenth.

And when you look at someone else's, you are looking at their fifteenth too.

The worst thing that happened to them this week isn't in there. Same as yours.

The argument at breakfast didn't get posted. Neither did the two hours awake at three in the morning.

You know all of this already. And you forget every bit of it the second you start scrolling.

This isn't saying everyone is faking it.

Picking the best shot is an ordinary thing to do. You do it. There's nothing wrong with it.

The problem starts one step later. When you hold their cut version up against your uncut one.

Yours has the unbrushed hair in it. Yours has all fourteen deleted shots still in it.

You lose that comparison every single time. And it was never a real one.

Next time somebody's life looks perfect, remember the fourteen you deleted. If you've ever taken ten to keep one, drop me a "same here".
