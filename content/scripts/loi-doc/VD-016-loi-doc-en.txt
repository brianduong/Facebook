"How are you?" — "I'm fine." That's where most conversations die.

Nobody has to think to answer that one. "Fine" is already loaded, before the question even arrives.

And you do it too. Someone asks, you say fine, and you weren't fine that morning at all.

Think of the person sitting two metres from you at work. For a year now you've asked each other that same question, every single morning.

Because the first question isn't really a question. It's a greeting, wearing a question mark to be polite.

The true answer only comes out for the second question. And the second question is the one almost nobody asks.

Not because you don't care. You're afraid one more question is prying into something they didn't offer.

So keep it short. Four words: "Are you really okay?"

Then watch for the pause. Half a second where they don't answer anything at all.

That pause is them deciding whether to tell you the real thing.

And the second question doesn't drag out some big secret. They just say the thing that's actually wearing them down.

You don't need an answer ready. Nobody is asking you to fix the thing they're carrying.

Just don't leave when the answer gets long. Staying is the whole of it.

If four words feel too heavy, swap them: "anything good this week?" Same door, opened gentler.

Today, with one person, ask one more question. Then come back and tell me what happened.
